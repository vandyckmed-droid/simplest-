How the colours and the scores are made

Score. Annualised return over annualised volatility, measured on a window with two edges. Window picks where it opens — 12 months back, 6 months back, or the mean of both windows' scores. Skip picks how far short of today it closes: 0, 10 or 21 trading days. The skip is the “−1” in 12–1, and it is worth being able to switch off — a fund can rank badly on a window that closed a month ago and still be up since.

Colour. Each fund ships 252 synchronised daily returns. Those give a 89 × 89 correlation matrix, turned into distances with √((1−ρ)/2) and grouped by k‑medoids: every fund sits with its nearest centre, and the centres move until the total within-group distance stops falling. The centre of each group is ringed in its row. Colour is anchored on the 8‑group run, so changing the count splits a group rather than repainting the page, and past eight groups the smallest wear a neutral grey — a ninth hue is never invented.

The dot beside a ticker means it correlates 0.60 or higher with something already in the basket — the question “do I own this already?”, asked pairwise rather than against the basket average.
